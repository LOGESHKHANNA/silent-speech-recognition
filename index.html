01 / THE PBL QUESTION

Can a machine read
what is not heard?

The project asks whether a machine learning model can recognize a limited set of spoken words or phrases from visible lip and facial movements without using an audible speech signal.

01
//

Speech can be a visual sequence.

Unlike conventional automatic speech recognition, the input here is visual rather than an audio waveform. This makes the problem relevant when audio is unavailable, noisy, privacy-sensitive or intentionally suppressed.

WHAT TO DISCUSS AFTER TESTING

Aggregate metrics are only one view.

The report notes that errors may come from visually similar words, video quality, lighting, head pose and speaker variation. Comparing the baseline with the refined CNN-LSTM approach should show whether temporal modeling and improved preprocessing provide a measurable benefit.

06 / LIMITATIONS/ /

Know the
boundary.

  • Selected vocabulary and available visual-speech dataset limit the task.
  • Performance can vary with speaker identity, head pose, illumination and camera quality.
  • Occlusion, facial appearance and visually similar words can affect recognition.
  • Real-time deployment may require model compression and efficient frame processing.
07 / FUTURE SCOPE++

Keep the
research moving.

  • Scale to a larger and more diverse multi-speaker dataset.
  • Extend recognition from isolated words to continuous silent speech.
  • Explore Transformer, 3D-CNN and hybrid architectures.
  • Improve robustness across lighting, camera angles and speakers.
  • Develop a real-time mobile or web-based demonstration.
